Plans and forecasts tend to be close to a best case. We look at the task from the inside, imagine the steps, and forget the many ways things go wrong. Kahneman tells how his own team, writing a curriculum, ignored what similar projects had taken and ended up taking years longer than planned.

The fix he proposes is the outside view: find a reference class of similar projects, use their real results as a baseline, and only then adjust for your case. He also likes the premortem: imagine the project has failed and write down why. Both work against the confident story System 1 builds ([[What you see is all there is]]) and against the first estimate that anchors the rest ([[Anchoring pulls estimates toward any number]]).

For my own work this means scheduling with real past data, which fits Newport's advice to plan time in blocks ([[Four philosophies for scheduling deep work]]).
