Verbs are where a sentence gets its energy. Zinsser prefers active verbs and the active voice, because they say who did what. The passive voice often hides the doer and makes the reader guess.

He is also hard on the helpers that pile up around weak verbs: adverbs that repeat what the verb already says, adjectives added out of habit, and little qualifiers like rather or sort of that soften every claim until nothing is claimed.

There is a link to Arendt here that I like. For her, deeds without a doer lose their meaning, and [[Speech reveals who someone is, not what they are|speech and action show who acts]]. The active voice keeps the who in the sentence.
