Most of us file notes by topic, in folders or under tags. Ahrens argues this is the wrong move. A topic decides in advance where an idea belongs, and then the idea only ever meets its neighbours in that folder.

Luhmann instead placed each new note behind a related note and linked it to others wherever they were. The index only gave a few entry points. The value sat in the links, because each link records a reason: this idea extends, contradicts or supports that one.

There is an echo here of Brown's finding that [[Interleaving helps learners tell problems apart]]. Mixing material from different areas makes you notice differences and similarities you would miss in blocked practice. Links across topics do the same for ideas, and they are how you build what Brown calls [[Mental models are built by extracting rules|mental models]].
