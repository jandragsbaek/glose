Copying a sentence from a book feels productive, but it skips the step where learning happens. When you have to say something in your own words, you find out right away whether you understood it. If you cannot rephrase it, you did not.

This lines up with what Brown reports from learning research. [[Rereading creates an illusion of knowing]] because familiar text feels understood. Producing your own version is harder, and that effort is what makes it stick, much like how [[Trying to answer before being taught helps learning]].

Rephrasing is also the first small act of [[Elaboration turns information into understanding|elaboration]]: you translate the author's idea into your own context and vocabulary, which makes it easier to link later. It is the practical reason to [[Collect ideas, not quotes]].
