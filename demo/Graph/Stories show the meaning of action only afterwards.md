The actor never fully knows what he is doing. The meaning of an action shows itself only when it is over, to the storyteller or historian who looks back. Action produces stories rather than products, and the story of a life has no author in the way a table has a maker.

Arendt opens her chapter on action with a line by Isak Dinesen about how sorrows can be borne when they are put into a story. Blixen's own book makes the same move: [[Fortællingen giver mening til det tilfældige]], and [[Erindringen ordner livet til en fortælling]]. Kahneman offers a more skeptical, psychological version in [[The experiencing self and the remembering self]]: the remembering self also keeps the story, not the lived moments.
