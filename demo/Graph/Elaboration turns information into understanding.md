Ahrens leans on learning research here: elaboration, meaning actively connecting new information to what you already know, beats rereading or highlighting for understanding and memory. You ask what the idea means, how it fits, what it contradicts, and where else it applies.

Writing a permanent note and then linking it is elaboration built into the routine. You cannot place a note in the box without deciding how it relates to other notes.

Brown makes the same point from another angle: [[Mental models are built by extracting rules]], and [[Reflection is a form of retrieval]]. Both describe the work of going back over material and asking what it means. The slip-box just gives that work a place to live. It starts with the simple step to [[Write notes in your own words]], and it pays off because [[Links between notes matter more than categories]].
