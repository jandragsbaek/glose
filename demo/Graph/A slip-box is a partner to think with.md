Luhmann described his slip-box as something like a conversation partner. Because notes are linked rather than filed by topic, the box can surprise you: you follow a link and find a note you forgot you wrote, now sitting next to a new idea in a way you did not plan.

This only works if the box is more than a storage system. A pile of excerpts cannot talk back. A network of short notes in your own words can, because [[Links between notes matter more than categories]] and the links carry your earlier reasoning.

I like this as a remedy for what Kahneman calls [[What you see is all there is]]. My head only works with what it has at hand right now. The box brings back things that are not at hand, and that is where new connections come from. It also explains why [[Structure grows from the bottom up]] instead of from a plan made in advance.
