When we describe someone, we usually list what they are: their qualities, talents, job, nationality. Arendt points out that in speaking and acting a person also discloses who they are, a unique identity that others often see more clearly than the person does. This who cannot be pinned down by a list of properties; it appears only in the shared space between people.

I connect this to Kierkegaard's idea that [[Selvet er et forhold, der forholder sig til sig selv|the self is a relation]], not a thing with fixed traits. It also explains why Zinsser insists that [[The writer's voice comes from being yourself]]: style is where the who of a writer shows, even on paper.
