In prospect theory, Kahneman and Tversky showed that we judge outcomes as gains and losses from a reference point, not as final states of wealth. And losses hurt more than equal gains please. Most people refuse a coin toss where they could lose 100 dollars unless the possible gain is much larger, often around twice as large.

This is System 1 at work ([[System 1 is fast and System 2 is slow]]), and the reference point acts much like an anchor ([[Anchoring pulls estimates toward any number]]). It explains why we hold on to bad projects, keep things we would never buy, and fight hard against a small cut.

I also see it in learning. A wrong answer feels like a loss, so we avoid tests that might show our errors, even though [[Mistakes are part of learning]]. Treating a mistake as information instead of a loss is one way to work against this bias.
