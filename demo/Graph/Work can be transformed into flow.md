Csikszentmihalyi found a paradox in his data: people report flow more often at work than in their free time, yet they say they would rather not be working. Work has the structure flow needs (goals, feedback, challenge), but we still see it as something forced on us.

His examples show that people can reshape even dull work. An assembly-line worker makes a game of beating his own best time and finds new ways to do each step. The task stays the same, but the goals and feedback are now his own ([[Clear goals and immediate feedback make flow possible]]).

This sits interestingly next to Arendt, who separates labor, work and action and warns about a life reduced to labor ([[Labor, work and action are the three activities of the vita activa]]). Flow suggests that how we engage changes the experience, even if not the category. Newport's [[Deep work is rare and valuable]] gives the knowledge-work version.
