Desirable difficulties is the psychologist Robert Bjork's term for obstacles that slow learning down in the moment but make it last. Spacing, interleaving, and trying to recall instead of rereading are all examples. They require more effort, and that effort strengthens the memory and connects it to what I already know.

The word desirable matters. A difficulty is only useful if I can overcome it. If I lack the background to make sense of the material at all, the struggle does not help. This reminds me of how [[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]]: too little challenge and nothing happens, too much and I give up.

It also runs against intuition. Kahneman shows that [[Cognitive ease feels like truth]], so the strategies that feel easy feel like they work. Here the feeling is a bad guide.
