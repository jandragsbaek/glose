Interleaving means mixing different kinds of problems or examples in one session instead of doing a block of one kind, then a block of the next. In the studies the book describes, learners who practiced in blocks did better during practice, but the ones who mixed did better on the later test.

The reason, as I understand it, is that blocked practice tells me which method to use before I start. Mixed practice makes me first decide what kind of problem this is. That skill of telling things apart is what I need in real life, where problems do not come labeled. It feeds directly into how [[Mental models are built by extracting rules]].

I see the same idea in Ahrens: [[Links between notes matter more than categories]]. Sorting notes into neat folders is like blocked practice. Linking a note to notes from other books forces me to compare, which is the useful part.
