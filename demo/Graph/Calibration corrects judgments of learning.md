Most of us are poor judges of what we know. The book describes several reasons: familiar material feels mastered, we remember our own fluency instead of our errors, and the least skilled are often the most confident (the Dunning-Kruger effect). Calibration is the habit of checking my judgment against something objective, such as a quiz, a test or a person who gives honest feedback.

Kahneman explains part of the problem: [[What you see is all there is]]. I judge my knowledge from what comes to mind, and I do not see the gaps. A test shows them.

Ahrens gives a version of this for writers: [[Writing gives feedback on what you have understood]]. If I cannot write it down clearly, I have not understood it yet, however sure I felt.
