Kahneman's acronym WYSIATI names a habit of System 1: it builds the best story it can from the information at hand and does not ask what is missing. A coherent story feels convincing, and the less we know, the easier it is to make one. Confidence comes from coherence, not from the amount or quality of evidence.

I see this in my own learning. I feel I understand a topic until a test shows the gaps, which is why [[Calibration corrects judgments of learning]]. It also shows up in planning ([[The planning fallacy makes plans too optimistic]]).

Reading other books in this Graph, I found the same blind spot in other forms. Kierkegaard writes about the despair of not even knowing you have a self ([[Uvidenhed om at have et selv er også fortvivlelse]]), and Blixen's narrator sees Africa through a colonist's frame without seeing the frame ([[Fortælleren ser Afrika med en kolonists blik]]).
