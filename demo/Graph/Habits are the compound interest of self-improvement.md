Clear's opening image: getting 1 percent better each day does not feel like anything on a given day, but over a year it multiplies into something large. The same math works in reverse for small bad habits. The point is not the exact number but the shape of the curve: the results of habits come late, and they come all at once.

This is why [[Systems matter more than goals]]. A goal is checked once; a habit is repeated hundreds of times, and the repetition is what compounds. It also explains why [[The plateau of latent potential hides early progress|the early phase feels useless]].

I see the same logic in note-taking: one note a day is nothing, but [[The slip-box makes the writing task small]] after a year. And in learning, [[Spaced practice beats massed practice]] is compound interest for memory.
