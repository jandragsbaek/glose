Autotelic comes from the Greek for self and goal: an activity that is its own goal. Some people seem to turn many situations into such activities. They set their own goals, pay close attention, and enjoy the task itself more than rewards or praise. Csikszentmihalyi calls this an autotelic personality and thinks it can be learned, at least in part.

Such people find flow in places others find dull, which is the theme of [[Work can be transformed into flow]]. They also handle free time better, because they do not wait for something outside to entertain them ([[Free time does not make people happy by itself]]).

I see a link to Clear's idea that lasting habits come from identity, not outcomes ([[Identity-based habits start with who you want to become]]). If I see myself as someone who likes the work, the reward is already inside the activity ([[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]]).
