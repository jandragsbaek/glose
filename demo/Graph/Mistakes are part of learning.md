Errors are not a sign of failure if I get feedback and correct them. The book argues that trying, getting it wrong, and then learning the right answer often leads to better learning than never making the mistake. The danger is fear: if I believe errors show a lack of ability, I avoid the difficult work that would make me better. The authors refer to Carol Dweck's research on growth mindset here.

Zinsser says the same about writing: [[Rewriting is the essence of writing]]. A first draft is supposed to be wrong in places.

Clear's rule to [[Never miss twice]] is the habit version: one slip is normal, the second one starts a pattern. And Arendt shows that [[Forgiveness and promising tame the consequences of action]]; without some way to release a mistake, nobody could dare to act. For learning, this is why [[Trying to answer before being taught helps learning]].
