Shallow work is the opposite of deep work: tasks that do not need much thinking, are often done while distracted, and do not create much new value. Email, most meetings, admin. Newport's test is how long it would take to train a smart graduate to do the task. If the answer is short, it is shallow.

Shallow work is not useless, but it is easy to replicate, so it is not where your value lies. Much of it is like what Arendt calls labor: it must be done again tomorrow and leaves nothing behind, see [[Labor answers to necessity and leaves nothing behind]]. The danger is that it fills the day because it is easy and visible: [[Busyness is a proxy for productivity]].

Newport suggests scheduling every minute of the day and asking managers for a shallow work budget. The goal is to protect time for [[Deep work is rare and valuable|deep work]], not to remove the shallow part entirely.
