Ahrens separates notes by what they are for:

- Fleeting notes are quick reminders of a thought. They are only useful if you process them within a day or so, then throw them away.
- Literature notes are short notes on what you read, in your own words, with the reference. They are selective, not complete.
- Permanent notes go into the slip-box. Each is written as if for a reader who does not know the context, and each holds [[One idea per note|one idea]].

Most people mix these up, so their archives fill with half-thoughts and copied passages. Keeping the jobs apart is what stops the box from turning into a junk drawer. It also protects against the trap Brown describes, that [[Rereading creates an illusion of knowing]]: a literature note forces you to say what the text means instead of looking at it again.
