Clear's rule for setbacks: missing once is an accident, missing twice is the start of a new habit. Everyone breaks a streak sometimes. What matters is getting back quickly, before the miss becomes a pattern.

I like that this rule forgives. It treats a slip as normal, not as proof that you failed. Arendt says that forgiveness is what allows people to act again after a mistake, instead of being bound to it forever: [[Forgiveness and promising tame the consequences of action]]. Clear's rule is a small, private version of that.

Brown makes the same case for learning: errors are not the opposite of progress, they are part of it. See [[Mistakes are part of learning]].
