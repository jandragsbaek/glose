I read this one in short bursts on my phone, which is a bit embarrassing given the topic. Newport's argument is simple and he repeats it often: the ability to focus without distraction on hard things is getting rarer just as it gets more valuable. [[Deep work is rare and valuable]] is the whole book in one sentence.

The first half is the case, the second half is the rules. The rules are where it got useful for me. [[Attention residue lowers performance after switching]] explained why my afternoons felt so thin: I was never fully on one thing. The [[A shutdown ritual ends the workday|shutdown ritual]] is the one habit I have actually kept.

I am more skeptical of the willpower parts. Newport treats willpower as a tank that runs dry, and that idea is disputed now (see [[Ego depletion is a disputed account of self-control]]). His advice still works, I think, but maybe for other reasons, closer to [[Environment design beats willpower]].

The social media chapter felt dated and a bit preachy, but [[The any-benefit approach to tools is a trap]] is a good test for any tool, not only social media. And [[Busyness is a proxy for productivity]] described my old job far too well.

It reads well next to Csikszentmihalyi. Deep work is roughly the working conditions for flow: [[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]].
