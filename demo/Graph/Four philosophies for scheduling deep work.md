Newport describes four ways to fit deep work into a life:

- Monastic: remove almost all shallow obligations (his example is Donald Knuth, who does not use email).
- Bimodal: split time into long deep periods and normal periods (Carl Jung's retreat).
- Rhythmic: a fixed daily habit, for example the same hours every morning.
- Journalistic: fit deep work in whenever a gap appears (Walter Isaacson).

He says the journalistic way is hard for beginners, because switching into deep mode takes practice. Rhythmic is the most realistic for most people.

The choice matters because people overestimate how much they will get done: [[The planning fallacy makes plans too optimistic]]. A fixed rhythm does not depend on that forecast. Clear makes the same argument: [[Systems matter more than goals]].
