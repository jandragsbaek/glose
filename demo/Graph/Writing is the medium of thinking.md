We usually picture writing as the last step: you research, you think, and then you write it down. Ahrens turns this around. Thinking does not happen before writing and then get copied onto paper; it happens in the writing. Our heads are bad at holding more than a few ideas at once, and paper is patient.

So every stage of intellectual work, from reading to drafting, should already produce written output. That is why [[A slip-box is a partner to think with]]: it is an outside space where thoughts can be held, compared and built on. Zinsser makes the mirror claim from the editor's side, that [[Clear thinking becomes clear writing]]. Newport's [[Productive meditation uses physical time for one problem|productive meditation]] is a useful counterweight: some thinking can happen on a walk, but it still needs to land on paper afterwards.
