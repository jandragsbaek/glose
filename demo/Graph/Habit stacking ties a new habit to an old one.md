Habit stacking is a special case of an implementation intention: instead of a time and place, the cue is a habit you already have. After I do the old thing, I will do the new thing. Clear credits the idea to BJ Fogg.

The old habit is already reliable, so the new one borrows its stability. The trick is to pick a cue that is specific and happens at the right moment. After I pour my coffee, I read one page. This is how I started reading every day.

Newport's [[A shutdown ritual ends the workday|shutdown ritual]] is a kind of stack: a fixed sequence of small steps, each one the cue for the next, ending with a closing phrase.
