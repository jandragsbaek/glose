Most people justify a tool (often social media) by pointing to any benefit it gives: I might miss something, I stay in touch. Newport calls this the any-benefit approach and says it ignores the costs, in time and attention. He suggests a craftsman approach instead: adopt a tool only if its benefits clearly outweigh its costs for the things that matter most to you.

The any-benefit thinking is a mental shortcut. We see the benefit easily, but the cost is spread out and invisible. Kahneman's [[What you see is all there is]] describes this well.

Changing which tools are around you is environment design: [[Environment design beats willpower]]. And the result of many low-value tools is often a full day with little done: [[Busyness is a proxy for productivity]].
