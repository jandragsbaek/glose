Kahneman describes thinking as two characters. System 1 works automatically and quickly, with no sense of effort: it reads a face, finishes a familiar phrase, or answers two plus two. System 2 is the slow, deliberate one that does long multiplication, follows an argument and checks rules. It feels like the real me, but most of the time it just signs off on what System 1 suggests ([[System 2 is lazy and accepts what System 1 offers]]).

He says himself that the two systems are useful fictions, not parts of the brain. I find the picture helpful anyway. It also explains why habits work: a cue that triggers a routine is System 1 at work, which is what [[The habit loop has four stages]] is built on. The cost is that System 1 produces confident answers from thin evidence ([[What you see is all there is]]) and treats familiar as true ([[Cognitive ease feels like truth]]).
