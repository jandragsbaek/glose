Because action happens inside a web of human relationships, every deed sets off reactions that reach far beyond what the actor intended. Arendt names three frustrations of action: its outcome is unpredictable, the process is irreversible, and its authors are often anonymous. Once something has been done among people, nobody can call it back.

This is why people have so often tried to replace action with the safer model of making, where one controls the result. Arendt's own answer is different: [[Forgiveness and promising tame the consequences of action]]. Blixen's account of losing her farm reads like a case study, where drought, falling prices and decisions made long before combine into an ending no one planned ([[Tabet af farmen former bogens slutning]]).
