Kahneman separates two selves. The experiencing self lives in the present and could answer the question: does it hurt now? The remembering self keeps score and tells the story afterwards. It is the remembering self that makes choices, because we choose between memories, not between experiences.

The two often disagree. A long painful procedure that ends gently can be remembered as less bad than a shorter one that ends sharply ([[The peak-end rule shapes how we remember]]).

This idea connects to several other books in this Graph. Arendt says the meaning of an action is only clear when the story is told ([[Stories show the meaning of action only afterwards]]), and Blixen's memoir is an example of memory ordering a life into a story ([[Erindringen ordner livet til en fortælling]]). Flow is interesting here: in the moment the experiencing self is fully absorbed, and only afterwards does the remembering self report that it was good ([[In flow the self disappears and returns stronger]]).
