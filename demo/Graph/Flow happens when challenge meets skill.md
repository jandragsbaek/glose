Flow is the state of full involvement where action and awareness merge and time seems to pass differently. Csikszentmihalyi found it in very different people: climbers, surgeons, chess players, workers. The common condition is a balance: the task asks a lot, but not more than you can give. When skills grow, the challenge has to grow with them, or flow turns into boredom ([[Flow lies between anxiety and boredom]]).

The structure of the activity matters as much as the person ([[Clear goals and immediate feedback make flow possible]]). Over time, the constant raising of the bar makes a person more skilled and more complex ([[Complexity grows through differentiation and integration]]).

Newport's [[Deep work is rare and valuable]] reads like a practical guide to setting up these conditions in knowledge work. And the stretch that flow needs looks a lot like the effort that makes learning stick ([[Desirable difficulties make learning stick]]).
