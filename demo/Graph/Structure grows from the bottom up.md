Students are often told to pick a topic first and then do the research. Ahrens thinks this is backwards. At the start you know too little to choose a good question, so the choice is mostly a guess.

With a slip-box you let the structure emerge. You read, take notes and link them, and over time clusters form where your interest and material are densest. Those clusters suggest the topic, and they already contain much of the argument. That is how [[The slip-box makes the writing task small]].

It reminds me of Csikszentmihalyi's idea that [[Complexity grows through differentiation and integration]]: a body of knowledge grows by adding distinct pieces and then connecting them, not by filling in an outline drawn in advance. It only works because [[Links between notes matter more than categories]] and because [[A slip-box is a partner to think with]].
