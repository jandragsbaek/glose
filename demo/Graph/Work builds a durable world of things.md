Work, in Arendt's sense, is fabrication: homo faber takes material from nature and makes objects that last, such as tables, houses, tools and works of art. Unlike [[Labor answers to necessity and leaves nothing behind|labor]], work has a clear beginning and end, it is guided by a model in the maker's mind, and its result stays in the world after the maker stops.

These durable things give human life a stable setting. We are born into a world that was there before us and will be there after us, and that shared world is what relates people to each other. Reading Blixen, I noticed the opposite case: on her farm it is the land, not the things people built, that feels permanent ([[Landskabet er en hovedperson]]). Arendt also warns against applying the means and ends thinking of work to [[Action is the only activity that happens directly between people|action]].
