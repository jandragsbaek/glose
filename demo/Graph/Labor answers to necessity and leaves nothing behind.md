For Arendt, labor is the activity that keeps the body alive: growing food, cooking, cleaning, the endless cycle of production and consumption. Its products are used up almost as soon as they are made, so labor never finishes and never leaves a lasting object behind. That is what she means by necessity: we do not choose it, life demands it.

This explains why labor can feel both essential and empty. It is not worthless, but it does not build anything that outlasts the effort, unlike [[Work builds a durable world of things|work]]. Kierkegaard describes a self that sees only necessity and no possibility in [[Nødvendighedens fortvivlelse mangler mulighed]], and a life reduced to labor comes close to that. The danger Arendt sees is a society where [[The modern age turns every activity into labor and consumption]].
