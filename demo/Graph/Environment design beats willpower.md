Clear argues that people with good self-control are often not stronger, they just spend less time in tempting situations. If you change the environment, the cues change, and so does the behavior. Put the water where the soda was; put the phone in another room.

This matters because relying on willpower is fragile. Kahneman shows how quickly effortful thinking gives up: [[System 2 is lazy and accepts what System 1 offers]]. The popular idea that willpower is a resource that runs out is disputed, though, see [[Ego depletion is a disputed account of self-control]]. Environment design works whether that theory is true or not, which is a strong argument for it.

Newport makes the same point for focus: [[Rituals reduce the willpower that deep work needs]]. A fixed place and fixed rules remove decisions.
