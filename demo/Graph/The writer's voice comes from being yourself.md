Zinsser says that what readers respond to most is the person behind the words: warmth, humanity, a sense that someone real is talking. You cannot get that by imitating a style you think sounds literary. It comes from relaxing into your own way of thinking and speaking, and from confidence that you have something to say.

Arendt offers a deeper version of the same idea: [[Speech reveals who someone is, not what they are]]. A voice is that disclosure on the page.

Clear's [[Identity-based habits start with who you want to become]] adds a practical angle: you grow into the writer you act as. And Kierkegaard's [[Fortvivlet ikke at ville være sig selv]] names the opposite, the refusal to be oneself. Bad prose that hides behind borrowed phrases is a small, everyday version of that.
