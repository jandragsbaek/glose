Csikszentmihalyi uses entropy for the state when information enters consciousness that conflicts with our goals. Worry, fear, envy and boredom all fit here. Attention is pulled in many directions, and the mind spends its energy on handling the disorder instead of acting ([[Attention is psychic energy]]).

The opposite is order in consciousness: attention lined up with a goal, which is what flow feels like ([[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]]). Without outside structure, the mind does not stay neutral. It drifts toward disorder, which is one reason why idle hours often feel bad ([[Free time does not make people happy by itself]]).

Newport's idea of [[Attention residue lowers performance after switching]] is a modern, measurable case of the same thing: every switch leaves bits of the last task in the mind, and that leftover noise is a small dose of psychic entropy.
