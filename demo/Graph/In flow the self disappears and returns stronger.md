In flow we lose self-consciousness. There is no room in attention to watch ourselves or worry about how we look, so the self, as an object we think about, drops out. Csikszentmihalyi stresses that this is not a loss. After the episode the self comes back richer, because it has stretched to meet a challenge ([[Complexity grows through differentiation and integration]]).

This reminds me of Kierkegaard's idea that the self is not a thing but a relation that relates to itself ([[Selvet er et forhold, der forholder sig til sig selv]]). In flow, that relation seems to rest for a while, and then it returns with more to relate to.

It also fits Kahneman's two selves: during flow the experiencing self is fully absorbed, and only the remembering self can later say how good it was ([[The experiencing self and the remembering self]]). People who seek this often are what he calls autotelic ([[The autotelic personality finds reward in the activity itself]]).
