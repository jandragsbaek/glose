The book's key claim is that attention is our most important resource. It is limited: we can only process a certain amount of information at a time, so what we pay attention to decides what our life contains. Csikszentmihalyi calls it psychic energy, because we can invest it, waste it, or let others take it.

This makes the quality of life partly a question of how attention is spent. Scattered attention gives disorder ([[Psychic entropy is disorder in consciousness]]); focused attention on a fitting challenge gives flow ([[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]]).

Kahneman agrees from another angle: effortful thinking draws on a limited budget, which is why [[System 2 is lazy and accepts what System 1 offers]]. And Newport turns the same idea into rules for work: [[Deep work is rare and valuable]] because focused attention is scarce, and [[The any-benefit approach to tools is a trap]] because every tool also takes a share of it.
