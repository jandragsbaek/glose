A permanent note should hold one idea, stated so it can stand on its own. This is less about tidiness and more about what the note can do. A note with three ideas can only be linked as a block, so its links become vague. A note with one idea can be placed exactly where it belongs and reused in many contexts.

Keeping notes small also forces a decision: what exactly is the point? That is close to Zinsser's rule that [[Every word must do work]]. A note with padding hides its idea, the same way [[Clutter is the disease of American writing|clutter]] hides a sentence's meaning.

The payoff comes later, because [[Links between notes matter more than categories]] and small notes make precise links possible. It is also what separates permanent notes from the other kinds in [[Fleeting, literature and permanent notes have different jobs]].
