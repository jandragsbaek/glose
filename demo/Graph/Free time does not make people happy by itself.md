One finding in the book surprised me. Free time is not automatically good time. In his studies, people often felt passive, weak and dull in leisure, especially in passive leisure such as watching television. Without goals or challenge, attention drifts and the mind fills with worry ([[Psychic entropy is disorder in consciousness]]).

Leisure needs skill too. Active hobbies, games, sports and conversation can produce flow; passive consumption rarely does ([[Attention is psychic energy]]).

Arendt makes a related, darker point: a society of laborers that gets more free time spends it on consumption, not on anything lasting ([[The modern age turns every activity into labor and consumption]]). And Newport warns that we confuse being busy with doing something worthwhile ([[Busyness is a proxy for productivity]]). Together these suggest the problem is not too much work or too little rest, but how attention is used in both.
