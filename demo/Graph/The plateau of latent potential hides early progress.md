Clear's image is an ice cube in a cold room. You warm the room one degree at a time and nothing happens, until it reaches the melting point. The earlier work was not wasted; it was stored. With habits, the gap between what you expect (steady progress) and what you see (nothing) is where most people quit. He calls this the valley of disappointment.

This is the emotional side of [[Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement]]: the curve is flat for a long time.

Ahrens describes the same with a slip-box. The first months give little back, but later the notes start to connect and [[The slip-box makes the writing task small]]. Brown adds that real learning often feels slow while it happens: [[Desirable difficulties make learning stick]].
