I picked this up because everyone uses the word flow and I wanted to know what the man who named it actually claimed. It is less a productivity book than I thought, and more a book about how to live. The central picture is easy to remember: you are in flow when the task asks just about as much as you can give ([[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]]). Too much and you get anxious, too little and you get bored.

What surprised me is how much of the book is about attention as a limited resource you spend, and about the quiet disorder that fills your head when nothing asks anything of you ([[Attention is psychic energy]], [[Psychic entropy is disorder in consciousness]]). It made me rethink my evenings. I had always assumed that more free time would make me happier, and his research says it often does not ([[Free time does not make people happy by itself]]).

The chapters on work were the most useful for me. Reading them next to Newport's [[Deep work is rare and valuable]] made both books clearer: deep work is one way to arrange your days so that flow can actually happen. Some passages feel dated and a bit grand, but the core idea has held up for me in practice.
