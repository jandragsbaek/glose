Clear argues that goals are about the result you want, while systems are the processes that lead there. Winners and losers often have the same goals, so the goal cannot be what makes the difference. A goal is also a single moment: once you reach it, the behavior that got you there can stop.

If you fix the system, the results follow on their own. This links to [[Identity-based habits start with who you want to become]], where the system is anchored in who you are rather than what you want to have.

Newport makes a similar move when he says you should choose a schedule before you rely on motivation: [[Four philosophies for scheduling deep work]]. And Kahneman gives a reason why goals mislead: [[The planning fallacy makes plans too optimistic]], while a system does not need a forecast to work.
