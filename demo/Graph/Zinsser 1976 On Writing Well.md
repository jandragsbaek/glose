A classic I had been putting off because I assumed it would be a list of rules. It is not, or not only. Zinsser writes like a friendly, slightly grumpy editor who has seen every bad habit and still likes writers.

The first chapters are the ones I keep going back to. [[Clutter is the disease of American writing]] is the line everyone remembers, and the cure he offers is plain: cut, then cut again. Behind it sits a bigger point, that [[Clear thinking becomes clear writing]]. If a sentence is muddy, the thought behind it usually is too. That connects straight to Ahrens and his claim that [[Writing is the medium of thinking]].

The book was revised many times over the years, and later parts on specific forms (travel, memoir, sports and so on) feel less relevant to me than the craft chapters. I skimmed some of them.

I am still pulling notes out of it. So far the most useful for my own work are [[Rewriting is the essence of writing]] and [[Every word must do work]]. The chapter on voice made me a bit uncomfortable, in a good way: I write like I am trying to sound like someone else.
