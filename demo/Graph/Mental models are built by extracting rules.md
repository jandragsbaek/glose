The book describes structure building: the ability to pick out the key ideas in new material and organize them into a coherent framework. Some learners do this well. Others hold on to too many details and miss the rule that ties them together. Learners who extract the underlying rule can apply it to new cases; learners who memorize examples cannot.

A mental model is the result: a set of connected rules that lets me act in a new situation. Mixing problem types helps, because [[Interleaving helps learners tell problems apart]] and so pushes me to look for what is common and what is different. [[Reflection is a form of retrieval]] does the same after the fact.

This matches Ahrens on [[Elaboration turns information into understanding]]. A note that says what an idea means and how it connects is a small step of structure building. So is letting [[Structure grows from the bottom up]] instead of sorting notes into fixed categories first.
