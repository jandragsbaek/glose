Writing a paper or a book feels huge when you start from a blank page. Ahrens's answer is that you never should. If you have been taking and linking notes all along, the draft is mostly a matter of arranging notes you already have, then rewriting them into a flowing text.

He also splits the work into separate tasks (reading, note-taking, linking, drafting, editing) that each get done on their own. Small tasks are easier to start and easier to finish, and an open task does not nag at you the way a vague big project does.

This is the same logic as Clear's [[Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement]]: one note a day looks like nothing, then adds up. It also explains [[The plateau of latent potential hides early progress|why the early months feel useless]]. And it makes Zinsser's claim that [[Rewriting is the essence of writing]] easier to live with, since you rewrite from material, not from nothing.
