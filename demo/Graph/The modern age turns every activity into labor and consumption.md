Arendt's diagnosis of modernity is that labor wins. In a society of jobholders, almost everything people do is understood as making a living, and the things we produce are consumed almost as quickly as food. Even durable objects become goods to be used up and replaced. The animal laborans, the human as laborer, becomes the model for everyone.

The striking consequence concerns free time. Arendt notes that the spare time of the laborer goes into more consumption, not into action or thought. Csikszentmihalyi reaches a similar result from his own research: [[Free time does not make people happy by itself]]. Newport's warning that [[Busyness is a proxy for productivity]] describes the same world from inside an office, where looking occupied replaces making something that lasts.
