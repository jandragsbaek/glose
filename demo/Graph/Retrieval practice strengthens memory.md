Pulling something out of memory changes the memory. Each time I recall a fact or a procedure, the route back to it gets stronger, and it is easier to find next time. This is the testing effect: a quiz is not only a way to measure learning, it is a way to cause it. Low-stakes quizzes, flashcards, and simply closing the book and asking myself what I just read all count.

The recall has to take some effort to do much good. That is why [[Spaced practice beats massed practice]] and why [[Rereading creates an illusion of knowing]]. I also think it explains part of [[The availability heuristic confuses ease of recall with frequency|why some things come to mind so readily]]: what I retrieve often becomes what is easy to retrieve, whether or not it is common.
