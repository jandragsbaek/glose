I expected a book about other people's mistakes and got a long, patient tour of my own. The frame is simple: a quick, automatic mind that jumps to answers and a slow, effortful one that should check them but mostly does not ([[System 1 is fast and System 2 is slow]]). After that, every chapter felt like catching myself in the act. I anchor on whatever number I saw last, I trust what comes to mind easily, and I build confident stories from the little I happen to know ([[What you see is all there is]]).

The part that stayed with me longest is the split between the self that lives through an experience and the self that remembers it. I notice it now when I look back on a holiday or a hard week: the ending decides almost everything ([[The peak-end rule shapes how we remember]]).

Not everything has aged well. The priming chapter and the material on ego depletion lean on studies that did not hold up when other labs tried them again, and I read those parts with a pencil in hand ([[Ego depletion is a disputed account of self-control]]). The core ideas on heuristics, loss aversion and planning still feel solid to me, and they changed how I plan projects and how I study.
