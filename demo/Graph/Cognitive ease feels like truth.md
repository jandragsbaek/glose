When something is easy to process, System 1 gives it a warm signal: familiar, fine, probably true. Repetition, a clear font, simple words, even a rhyme all make a statement feel more believable. The feeling of ease is real, but it says nothing about whether the claim is correct ([[System 2 is lazy and accepts what System 1 offers]]).

This explains a trap in studying. Reading the same chapter again makes it feel familiar, and familiar feels like knowing ([[Rereading creates an illusion of knowing]]). Strain, on the other hand, makes us more careful, which fits with [[Desirable difficulties make learning stick]].

There is a good side for writers. If ease makes readers trust and follow you, then plain, simple prose is not only polite but persuasive. That is one more reason why [[Simplicity is the core of good writing]].
