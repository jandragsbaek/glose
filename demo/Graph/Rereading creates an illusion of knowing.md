Rereading is the most common study strategy and one of the least effective. The problem is not that it does nothing, but that it feels like much more than it is. On the second or third pass the text is familiar, it reads smoothly, and I take that fluency as a sign that I know the material. Then I close the book and cannot explain it.

Kahneman describes the same trap from another side: [[Cognitive ease feels like truth]]. What is easy to process feels right and feels known. The fix is to check myself instead of trusting the feeling. [[Retrieval practice strengthens memory]] and also shows me what I actually know, which is the basis of [[Calibration corrects judgments of learning|calibration]].

For note-taking this is the argument for Ahrens' rule to [[Write notes in your own words]]. Copying a passage is rereading with a pen.
