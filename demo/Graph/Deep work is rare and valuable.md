Newport's central hypothesis: the ability to do deep work (focused, distraction-free work that pushes your abilities to their limit) is becoming rarer at the same time as it becomes more valuable. The few who build this skill will do well.

He gives two reasons it is valuable: you need it to learn hard things quickly, and you need it to produce work at a high level. It is rare because modern work pulls the other way, toward [[Shallow work is easy to replicate|shallow work]] and constant connection.

Deep work looks a lot like the working conditions for flow: [[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]]. And Csikszentmihalyi's view that [[Attention is psychic energy]] gives a reason why focus is valuable: it is the limited resource that all thinking runs on.
