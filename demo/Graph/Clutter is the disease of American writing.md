Zinsser's best-known claim: most writing is buried under words that do nothing. Long words where short ones would do, phrases like at this point in time instead of now, qualifiers that soften every claim, jargon that sounds important. Each piece seems harmless, but together they hide the meaning.

His cure is to strip each sentence to its cleanest parts and to treat every extra word as a suspect.

Kahneman gives this a nice backing: [[Cognitive ease feels like truth]], and his book mentions research showing that needlessly complex language makes writers seem less intelligent, not more. Clutter costs the reader effort and gains the writer nothing.

The same pressure works on notes. [[One idea per note]] is a kind of anti-clutter rule for thinking. See also [[Every word must do work]] and [[Simplicity is the core of good writing]].
