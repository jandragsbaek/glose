The well-known diagram in the book has challenge on one axis and skill on the other. When the challenge is too high for my skill, I feel anxious. When it is too low, I get bored. Flow is the channel between them ([[Flow happens when challenge meets skill]]).

The channel moves. As I practice, my skill grows, the same task becomes boring, and I must look for a harder one to get back into flow. This is how flow pushes growth ([[Complexity grows through differentiation and integration]]).

Boredom here is a signal, not only a feeling to escape. Newport's advice to [[Embrace boredom to train concentration]] fits well: if I always flee boredom with my phone, I never learn to stay with a task long enough to find its challenge. Both anxiety and boredom are forms of disorder in the mind ([[Psychic entropy is disorder in consciousness]]).
