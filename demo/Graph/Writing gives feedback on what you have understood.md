Reading gives a weak signal of understanding. Everything seems clear while your eyes are on the page. Writing gives a strong signal: when you try to explain an idea in a note, gaps show up at once.

Ahrens treats this as one of the main reasons to write all the time. It turns a vague feeling of knowing into a test you can fail. That is what Brown means by [[Calibration corrects judgments of learning]]: you replace your impression with evidence.

Csikszentmihalyi would add that feedback is what makes an activity absorbing. [[Clear goals and immediate feedback make flow possible]], and a single note is a small, clear task with feedback built in. Seen this way, [[Writing is the medium of thinking]] and its own check on that thinking, as long as you [[Write notes in your own words]].
