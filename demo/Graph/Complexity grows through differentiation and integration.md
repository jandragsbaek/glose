For Csikszentmihalyi, a complex self has two features. Differentiation: it is unique, with its own skills and goals. Integration: it is connected to other people and to ideas larger than itself. Too much differentiation makes a person self-centered; too much integration makes them lose themselves. Growth needs both at once.

Flow drives this growth. Each time we meet a harder challenge, we become more differentiated, and the order we create links our experiences together ([[In flow the self disappears and returns stronger]], [[Flow lies between anxiety and boredom]]).

I see the same pattern in Kierkegaard, who describes the human being as a synthesis of opposites that must be held together ([[Mennesket er en syntese af det uendelige og det endelige]]). And in learning: Brown's [[Mental models are built by extracting rules]] describes how separate facts are first told apart and then joined into a larger structure, much like [[Structure grows from the bottom up]].
