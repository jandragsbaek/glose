Cramming works for tomorrow and fails for next month. When I practice the same thing many times in a row, I get fast gains that feel convincing, but they fade. When I spread the same amount of practice over days, I have forgotten a little between sessions, so each [[Retrieval practice strengthens memory|act of recall]] takes more effort. That effort, and the time for the memory to consolidate, is what makes it last.

The cost is that spaced practice feels less productive while I do it. This is one of the clearest cases of [[Desirable difficulties make learning stick]].

It looks a lot like Clear's point that [[Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement]]: small sessions, repeated over time, add up to more than one big push. Spacing and [[Interleaving helps learners tell problems apart|interleaving]] also go well together, since mixing topics spaces each one out automatically.
