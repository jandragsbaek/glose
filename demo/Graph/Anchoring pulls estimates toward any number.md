Any number we see before an estimate drags the estimate toward it, even when we know the number is random. In one classic study, people spun a wheel that was rigged to stop on a low or a high number, and then guessed a quantity that had nothing to do with the wheel. Those who saw the high number guessed higher.

> [!ai]
> Kahneman explains anchoring in two ways. System 2 adjusts away from the anchor but stops too early, and System 1 primes ideas that fit the anchor ([[System 1 is fast and System 2 is slow]]). Knowing about the effect does not remove it.

For planning, this matters a lot: the first estimate said in a meeting becomes the anchor for everything after it ([[The planning fallacy makes plans too optimistic]]). In study, a first guess about how well I know something anchors my confidence too, so I need tests to check it ([[Calibration corrects judgments of learning]]).
