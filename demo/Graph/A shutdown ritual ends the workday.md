At the end of each workday, Newport goes through a fixed routine: check email one last time, write down every open task, review the plan for the next days, and then say a set phrase to mark the end of work. After that, he does not think about work.

The reason: unfinished tasks keep pulling at your attention (he mentions the Zeigarnik effect). Writing a plan for them is enough to let the mind relax. This also prevents [[Attention residue lowers performance after switching|attention residue]] from leaking into the evening, where the brain needs rest to recover.

Ahrens makes a similar claim: a trusted external system frees the mind from holding everything, see [[A slip-box is a partner to think with]]. The ritual itself is a stack of habits, like [[Habit stacking ties a new habit to an old one]].
