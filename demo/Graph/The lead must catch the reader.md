The first sentence of a piece matters most. If it does not make the reader want the second, there will be no second. Then the second has to lead to the third, and so on. Zinsser treats the whole piece as a chain of small persuasions to keep reading.

He gives similar care to the ending: stop when you are done, ideally on something that surprises or rounds things off, rather than with a summary that repeats everything.

Csikszentmihalyi's point that [[Attention is psychic energy]] explains the stakes: readers spend a limited resource on you. And Kahneman's [[The peak-end rule shapes how we remember|peak-end rule]] suggests the ending will shape how the whole piece is remembered, which backs Zinsser's care for the last sentence.
