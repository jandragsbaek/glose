It is tempting to fill a notebook with striking passages. Ahrens warns that an archive of quotes is mostly useless later: the quotes have lost their context, they are in someone else's words, and you still have to do the thinking you skipped.

What you want to keep is the idea and what it means for your own questions. A short quote is fine when the exact wording matters, but the note around it should be yours.

This links to Brown's warning that [[Rereading creates an illusion of knowing]]. A collected quote feels like knowledge because it is familiar, a bit like how [[Cognitive ease feels like truth]]. Turning it into an idea in [[Write notes in your own words|your own words]] is what tests whether you understood it. It also explains why [[Fleeting, literature and permanent notes have different jobs]]: the literature note is where the quote turns into an idea.
