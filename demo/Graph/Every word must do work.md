This is my short summary of Zinsser's method rather than a line from the book. Every word in a sentence should earn its place. If you can remove it without losing meaning, remove it. Long words should only be used when no short word will do, and words that add tone without content (very, really, quite) should usually go.

It is a strict test, and applying it is most of what [[Rewriting is the essence of writing|rewriting]] consists of. It also pushes you to [[Strong verbs and the active voice carry a sentence|choose strong verbs]] instead of propping up weak ones.

Ahrens's rule of [[One idea per note]] is the same test applied to thinking: every note must do work in the network. Padding a note hides its idea just as padding a sentence does, which is why [[Clutter is the disease of American writing]].
