The reader's attention is short and easy to lose. Zinsser says that when a reader gives up, it is usually because the writer was careless: a confusing sentence, a missing step, a word that means two things. The reader should not have to work out what you meant.

Simplicity here is not dumbing down. It is respect for the reader and discipline for the writer. It comes from deciding what you think and then saying it as plainly as possible, which is why [[Clear thinking becomes clear writing]].

Kahneman's [[Cognitive ease feels like truth|work on cognitive ease]] helps explain why this works so well: text that is easy to process is not only read but also believed more readily. That cuts both ways, but for an honest writer it is a reason to cut [[Clutter is the disease of American writing|clutter]] and make [[Every word must do work]].
