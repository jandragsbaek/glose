Newport draws on research by Sophie Leroy: when you switch from task A to task B, part of your attention stays with A. This residue lowers performance on B, especially if A was not finished. A quick look at email in the middle of focused work is enough to cause it.

This is why multitasking feels productive but is not. Every small check costs more than its few seconds.

Csikszentmihalyi would call this disorder in attention: [[Psychic entropy is disorder in consciousness]]. Unfinished tasks pull at the mind. Newport's answer is to finish the day cleanly with [[A shutdown ritual ends the workday]], and to train the mind to stay with one thing: [[Embrace boredom to train concentration]].
