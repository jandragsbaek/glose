The book calls this generation: trying to answer a question or solve a problem before anyone shows me how. Even when my attempt is wrong, the later explanation lands better, because I have already searched my memory and found out what I was missing. The answer then fills a gap I can feel.

Two conditions make it work. I need to actually try, not just glance at the question, and I need to get the correct answer afterwards. Without feedback, a wrong guess can stick. With it, [[Mistakes are part of learning]].

This is close to why Ahrens wants me to [[Write notes in your own words]]. Writing my own version of an idea is a small act of generation, and it shows me where my understanding breaks. It is one of the clearest [[Desirable difficulties make learning stick|desirable difficulties]].
