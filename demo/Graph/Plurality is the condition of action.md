Plurality means that people, not one abstract Man, live on the earth and inhabit the world. We are all the same kind of being, yet no person is ever identical to anyone who lived before or will live after. Arendt calls this the condition of action: without equality we could not understand each other, and without distinction we would have nothing to say.

Action and speech therefore need an audience of equals who are also different. Alone, one can labor or make things, but one cannot act. Blixen's farm, with Kikuyu, Maasai, Somali and European people living side by side, is a vivid picture of plurality, although the colonial order kept them far from equal ([[Farmen er et fællesskab af mange folk]]).
