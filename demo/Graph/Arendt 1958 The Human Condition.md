I came to this book expecting political theory and found something closer to a map of my own days. Arendt splits the active life into labor, work and action, and once I had the words I kept sorting my calendar by them. Most of what fills a week is labor in her sense: it keeps things going and then disappears. [[Labor, work and action are the three activities of the vita activa]] became the hub of my notes.

The part that stayed with me longest is the chapter on action. Acting among other people is fragile: you can't predict it and you can't take it back. Her answer, forgiveness and promising, felt surprisingly practical, almost like advice for a team. I also loved discovering that she opens that chapter with a line by Isak Dinesen, which sent me straight to [[Blixen 1937 Den afrikanske farm]].

It is not an easy read. The last part on the modern age is dense, and I'm not sure I followed every turn of the argument about world alienation. But the idea that each of us can begin something new is one I want to keep close: [[Natality means every person can begin something new]].
