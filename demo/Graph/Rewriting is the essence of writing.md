Zinsser insists that good writing is rewritten writing. Professional writers go over their sentences many times: tightening, rearranging, cutting. The first draft is not the finished product, and expecting it to be is what makes writing feel impossible.

I find this freeing. A clumsy first draft is not failure, it is raw material. Brown makes the same case for learning: [[Mistakes are part of learning]] and the effort of correcting them is where improvement comes from.

Ahrens adds a practical support. If [[The slip-box makes the writing task small]], you are rarely staring at a blank page, so more of your time goes to rewriting and less to producing the first words. Most of what rewriting does is removing [[Clutter is the disease of American writing|clutter]]. It is also the clearest case of how [[Writing is a craft learned by doing]].
