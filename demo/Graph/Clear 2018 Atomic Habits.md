I expected a self-help book with a lot of padding, and parts of it are exactly that. But the core idea stuck with me more than I wanted it to: small changes do not look like anything for a long time, and then they do. [[Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement]] is the note I keep coming back to, together with [[The plateau of latent potential hides early progress]].

What I liked most is that Clear does not ask for more discipline. He asks you to change the setup. [[Environment design beats willpower]] and [[The two-minute rule makes starting easy]] are practical in a way most advice is not. I tried habit stacking with my reading (one page after the morning coffee) and it actually held for weeks.

I am less sure about the science in places. The book leans on studies that are cited in a popular way, and some of the self-control research behind this genre is disputed (see [[Ego depletion is a disputed account of self-control]]). The four laws are a neat frame, but they are a frame, not a theory.

The identity chapter surprised me. The idea that each action is a vote for who you are made me think of Kierkegaard, of all people: [[Identity-based habits start with who you want to become]] is a much lighter version of the question of becoming a self. Compare [[Fortvivlet ikke at ville være sig selv]].
