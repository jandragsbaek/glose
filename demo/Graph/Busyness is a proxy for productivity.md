In knowledge work, it is hard to measure what someone produces. Without a clear measure, people fall back on what is visible: answering email fast, attending meetings, being busy in public. Newport calls this busyness as a proxy for productivity.

It explains why [[Shallow work is easy to replicate]] still fills the day. It looks like work, and it feels like work, but it creates little of value.

Arendt saw a larger version of this: modern society treats every activity as a job to keep going, valued for the activity itself and its consumption, not for what it leaves behind, see [[The modern age turns every activity into labor and consumption]]. Csikszentmihalyi adds that more free time does not fix this on its own: [[Free time does not make people happy by itself]].
