I picked this up because everyone who builds note apps seems to quote it, and I wanted to see what the fuss was about. The core is simple: Niklas Luhmann kept a box of small, numbered, linked notes for decades and wrote an absurd amount from it. Ahrens turns that into a method and argues for it with learning research.

What stuck with me most is the claim that [[Writing is the medium of thinking]], not something you do after the thinking is finished. That changes what a note is for. A note is not storage, it is a step in an argument you have not written yet.

The practical parts are good. The split into fleeting, literature and permanent notes finally made sense to me, and so did [[Links between notes matter more than categories]]. I was a folder person. I am less of one now.

The book is repetitive in places and a bit too sure that one method fits everyone. But the idea that [[The slip-box makes the writing task small]] is exactly what I needed to hear. It also pairs well with what Brown says about rereading, and with Zinsser on [[Clear thinking becomes clear writing]].
