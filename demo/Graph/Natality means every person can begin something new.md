Arendt grounds action in the fact of birth. Every human being comes into the world as a newcomer, and with each birth something unique enters it. That is why people are capable of initiative: to act is to begin, to set something in motion that was not there before. She even describes natality as the miracle that saves the world of human affairs from its natural ruin.

Natality is a hopeful counterweight to a philosophy built around mortality. It also speaks to Kierkegaard: [[Mulighedens fortvivlelse mangler nødvendighed]] warns against possibility without grounding, while [[Troen er fortvivlelsens modsætning]] describes a self that rests in the power that established it, for whom everything is possible. Arendt's version is political rather than religious, but both insist that a person is more than the product of circumstances.
