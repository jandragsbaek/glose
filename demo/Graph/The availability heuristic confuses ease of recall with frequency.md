When we judge how common something is, we often ask an easier question instead: how easily do examples come to mind? Vivid, recent or dramatic events are easy to recall, so we overrate them. After a plane crash is in the news, flying feels more dangerous for a while, although nothing about the real risk has changed.

A study Kahneman describes makes it stranger: people asked to list many examples of their own assertive behavior rated themselves as less assertive than people asked for only a few. The struggle to recall the later examples felt like evidence. Ease is the signal, not the count ([[Cognitive ease feels like truth]]).

This links to learning in a useful way. What is easy to recall is what has been recalled often, and [[Retrieval practice strengthens memory]] is a way to choose on purpose which things become available. Without that, my mind fills the gaps with whatever is at hand ([[What you see is all there is]]).
