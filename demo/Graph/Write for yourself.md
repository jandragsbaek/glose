Zinsser says not to write for an imagined mass audience. Nobody can picture that reader, and trying to please everyone produces bland prose. Write to please yourself, and trust that readers worth having will come along.

He separates two things. Craft is about the reader: you must make the text clear and easy to follow, which is why [[The lead must catch the reader]]. Attitude is about you: what interests you, what you find funny, what you care about. The attitude part is yours alone.

This connects to Csikszentmihalyi's [[The autotelic personality finds reward in the activity itself|autotelic personality]]: if the reward is in the writing itself, you are less at the mercy of outside approval. It also ties in with Clear's point that [[Identity-based habits start with who you want to become]]. Writing for yourself is a way of acting as a writer, not as someone seeking an audience.
