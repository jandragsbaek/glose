When we look back on an experience, we do not add up every moment. Memory is mostly shaped by two points: the most intense moment and the end. Length hardly counts, which Kahneman calls duration neglect. In the cold-water experiments, many people chose to repeat a longer trial that ended slightly less painfully over a shorter one, because they remembered the longer one as better.

This is the remembering self at work ([[The experiencing self and the remembering self]]), and it shows how much a story depends on its ending ([[Stories show the meaning of action only afterwards]], [[Erindringen ordner livet til en fortælling]]).

I use it in a small way. If the end of a workday shapes how I remember it, then a calm closing routine is worth the few minutes it costs ([[A shutdown ritual ends the workday]]).
