In the book, Kahneman presents ego depletion as solid science: self-control draws on a limited resource, and after one effortful task people give up sooner on the next. He builds on Baumeister's work and links it to the lazy System 2 ([[System 2 is lazy and accepts what System 1 offers]]).

Since then, large replication efforts across many labs found the effect to be very small or absent. The idea is now strongly disputed, and I treat that chapter as a hypothesis, not a fact. Kahneman himself later said in public that he had trusted small studies too much in his chapter on priming, which is a related problem.

The practical advice still holds for other reasons: do not make important tasks depend on willpower in the moment ([[Environment design beats willpower]], [[Rituals reduce the willpower that deep work needs]]). This is a good reminder to check popular claims about the mind, as with [[Learning styles are not supported by evidence]].
