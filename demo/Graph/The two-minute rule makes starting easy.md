When you start a new habit, shrink it until it takes less than two minutes. Not read a book, but read one page. Not run three miles, but put on running shoes. Clear's point is that a habit must be established before it can be improved, and the hardest part is showing up.

It sounds almost silly, but it works because starting is where most habits fail. Once the first step is automatic, the rest often follows.

Newport has a parallel in his rituals: lowering the friction of getting into deep work, see [[Rituals reduce the willpower that deep work needs]]. And Zinsser's view that [[Writing is a craft learned by doing]] is the same idea at a larger scale: you get better by doing small amounts, often, not by waiting for the big session.
