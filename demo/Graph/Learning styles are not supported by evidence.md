Many people believe that each of us learns best in one style (visual, auditory and so on) and that teaching should match it. The book is direct about this: people do have preferences, but there is little scientific support for the claim that matching instruction to a preferred style improves learning. The authors point to a large review of the research (by Pashler and colleagues) that found almost no studies designed to test the claim properly, and the few that were did not support it.

The authors suggest it is more useful to match the teaching to the material, and to look at differences that do matter, such as how well a person extracts the key ideas. See [[Mental models are built by extracting rules]].

Why does the myth last? I think because [[Cognitive ease feels like truth]]: it is a flattering, simple story. That is a good reason to [[Calibration corrects judgments of learning|check my beliefs against evidence]].
