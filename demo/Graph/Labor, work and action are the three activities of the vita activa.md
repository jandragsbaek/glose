Arendt divides the active life, the vita activa, into three basic activities, each tied to a condition of human existence:

- [[Labor answers to necessity and leaves nothing behind|Labor]] corresponds to life itself, the biological process of the body.
- [[Work builds a durable world of things|Work]] corresponds to worldliness, the artificial world of objects we make.
- [[Action is the only activity that happens directly between people|Action]] corresponds to plurality, the fact that we live among others.

The distinction is useful outside philosophy too. When I look back at a day, I can ask which of the three I actually did. Csikszentmihalyi's claim that [[Work can be transformed into flow]] reads differently once you notice that his idea of work mixes Arendt's labor and work. Her later argument, that [[The modern age turns every activity into labor and consumption]], only makes sense against this threefold scheme.
