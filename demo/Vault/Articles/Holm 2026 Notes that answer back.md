# Notes that answer back

> A sample web essay for the Glose demo. The author and the site are made up.

Most notes are written once and never read again. They sit in a folder as a record that the reading happened, not as something that changes how we think. ==A note earns its place only when it can answer back==[^glose-1]: when, months later, it disagrees with what we are about to write.

Three habits make that more likely. Each costs a little time while reading and pays it back while writing.

## Write the claim, not the topic

A note titled "Memory" holds nothing you can argue with. A note titled "Recall beats rereading for learning" holds a claim, and a claim can be tested, linked and contradicted. When you finish a passage, ask what it says, not what it is about.

==The title is the smallest summary you will ever write==, so spend the most care on it.

## Link while the reading is fresh

A link made months later is a guess. A link made while you read records why two ideas met in your head. Write one sentence on every link: why this note belongs beside that one.

## Read the note before you write the essay

Before you draft, read the notes the essay will draw on, and let them object. If a note no longer convinces you, that is not a failure of the note. It is the note doing its work.

[^glose-1]: Same idea as the Probe: a note that cannot be questioned teaches nothing.
